# Book of Adria: A Diablo Bestiary

*Diablo's witch Adria "writes" a field guide to the monsters of Sanctuary, in a lore-and-art hardcover.*

## In short

This is an official Blizzard hardcover (2018) by Robert Brooks and Matt Burns, written in-universe as Adria's own notes on the demons, tribes and beasts of the Diablo world. It's a **lore and art reference book to dip into**, not a story or a guide to playing the game. **Verdict: worth it for Diablo fans, especially Diablo III players. Skip it if you want deep story or Diablo I/II coverage.**

## Verdict

**Worth it for a Diablo fan who likes artwork and lore.** Reviewers say it's less lore-heavy than the Book of Tyrael or Book of Lorath, and focused on Diablo III. At launch it was about $30 on Blizzard's store and $19.49 on Amazon. *Confidence: medium (few independent reviews).*

## What it is

I identified it from the cover (title, subtitle and Diablo logo). The photo is a cover image only, with no price or store tag, so I can't say which edition or where it was sold. It's the third book in Blizzard's Horadric Vault series, after the Book of Cain and Book of Tyrael. Published December 2018.

**Type: art / lore reference book (bestiary).**

## What's inside

- A **bestiary written as Adria's notes**: entries on corrupted beasts, tribes and demons across the franchise.
- It **opens with Adria's history in the Coven** (going back to the Sin Wars), then moves through events leading up to Reaper of Souls. Some entries note how creatures can be used in magical practice.
- **Lots of illustrations.** One retailer describes it as "equal parts tome of lore and beautifully illustrated art book."
- **Page count is unclear:** listings give 144 or 244 pages. Check the publisher's listing.

**How you'd use it:** flip through for the art and read entries one at a time. It's not meant to be read straight through like a novel.

## What people say

- **Diablofans** gave a positive review: if you liked the Book of Cain or Book of Tyrael you'll love this one, and the final section is stronger than anything in those books. Praised as a more coherent collection.
- **Buyers** praise the artwork and call it a good companion for Diablo III.
- **Critics:** a Goodreads reviewer said the switch to a bestiary lost what made the earlier books fun, and that there aren't enough entries to justify the lore. A buyer noted it's almost entirely Diablo III, with little from games I and II.
- **Reddit:** no threads found.
