# The Ultimate RPG Worldbuilding Deck

*A flip-top box of 75 prompt cards that help a game master invent the people, places, history and rumors of a fantasy world.*

## In short

This is a 75-card prompt deck by James D'Amato (Adams Media / Simon & Schuster, 2025) for **fantasy role-playing games like D&D**. You draw a card and answer its questions, alone or with your players, to flesh out your game world. It's a **prep and brainstorming tool, not a game or a rulebook.** **Verdict: a cheap, useful tool for game masters who want quick, play-focused worldbuilding. Skip it if you want maps, rules or a ready-made setting.**

## Verdict

**Worth it for a game master (or solo worldbuilder) at around the $18.99 list price.** The one detailed review I found is positive, but the reviewer got a free copy from the publisher, and I found no independent reviews of the deck. *Confidence: medium. Contents are solid, opinion is thin.*

## Who it's for

- **Good for:** game masters who want fast, playable detail for towns, factions and backstories. New GMs, and anyone who gets stuck staring at a blank page. Also a good gift for a GM, or a fantasy writer who builds worlds.
- **Skip if:** you want maps, stat blocks, random tables or a full ready-made setting. If you'd rather use a free AI chatbot for ideas, that's an alternative (the reviewer argues the deck gives more personal, play-focused prompts).

## What's inside

- **75 cards** in a **fold-up deck box** with a flip-top lid. The cards are tarot-sized, per one review.
- **Five color-coded categories:** People, Places, History, Institutions, and Rumors.
- **What each card does:** it poses a prompt. Per the back cover, these are **"seventy-five all-new prompts"** based on D'Amato's *The Ultimate RPG Game Master's Worldbuilding Guide*, with **multiple-choice and fill-in-the-blank exercises**. The box says it works for **interactive or solo** worldbuilding for any fantasy RPG.

**Flip-through (from review snippets, not photos; I couldn't open the review pages themselves):**

- **Card count:** one retailer lists **75 interactive cards plus 4 instruction cards**.
- **The five types:** People (allies and antagonists), Institutions (factions PCs can deal with), Places (locations to explore), History (events and culture), and Rumors (unreliable stories that point to things for the PCs to chase).
- **Place cards:** the top half gives a prompt and physical details (one example mentioned is a rest stop on the road). The bottom half asks what the place means to the player characters and what gameplay event will happen there.
- **People cards:** a short paragraph with blanks to fill in. Some cards ask for two positive and two negative things about a person or place.
- **Extras:** reviewers mention "prompts for the prompts" on the cards, and a hint of randomness that can push you to improvise. The cards are larger than a standard playing card.
- It does **not** help you draw maps.

## Series and what comes next

It's part of James D'Amato's **Ultimate Role Playing Game series** (Adams Media). It works on its own, and the box says the prompts are all new. The related titles I found, roughly in release order:

- **The Ultimate RPG Character Backstory Guide** (2018)
- **The Ultimate RPG Gameplay Guide** (2019)
- **The Ultimate RPG Game Master's Worldbuilding Guide** (2021, 272 pages, foreword by Patrick Rothfuss): the book this deck's prompts are based on
- **The Ultimate RPG Guide Boxed Set** (2021): the three books above together
- **The Ultimate RPG Campfire Card Deck** (2023)
- **The Ultimate RPG Game Master's Guide** (March 2024)
- **The Ultimate RPG Villain Backstory Guide**: listed for March 2026

An RPG Tarot Deck also exists. Some dates differ between sources.
