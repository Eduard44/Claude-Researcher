# The Art of Nothing: 25 Years of Mutts and the Art of Patrick McDonnell

*A big landscape art book about the making of the "Mutts" comic strip, not a collection of strips.*

## In short

This is a 2019 Abrams ComicArts hardcover (240 pages) celebrating 25 years of Patrick McDonnell's comic strip *Mutts*. It's a **behind-the-scenes art retrospective**: sketches, process, unpublished work, commentary, a foreword by Eckhart Tolle, and an interview with Lynda Barry. **Verdict: worth it if you already love Mutts or enjoy cartooning craft. If you want the strips themselves, start with a regular Mutts collection.**

## Verdict

## What's inside

- **Process and sketches:** roughs, sketchbook pages, proposals and unpublished artwork, plus published strips and paintings.
- **Commentary from McDonnell** about his influences and how he works, with some autobiographical notes.
- **Tolle's foreword** (listings also call it an introduction) and a **career-spanning interview** where Lynda Barry is credited with moderating/commentary. Listings differ on exactly how.
- **Format:** 240 pages, large landscape hardcover (about 12" x 9"), black-and-white and color.
- It's a sequel to a 2003 McDonnell monograph, per Library Journal.

**How you'd use it:** browse for the art and read the process commentary. It's not a "read the comic" book.

## What people say

- **Positive:** "a mostly wonderful book about the career of MUTTS creator Patrick McDonnell"; praised for the warmth, the animal-love in the work, and the look at McDonnell's mind. Library Journal calls it a lavish display of his art.
- **Mixed:** one reader found it "not much to brag about" and was put off by the eastern-mysticism tone. Another found it a bit tepid next to his other work.
- **Tip from a reviewer:** new readers should start with a Mutts strip collection to get used to his style.
